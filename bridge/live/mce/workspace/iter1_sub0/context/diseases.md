# Condition Profiles — Symptom Diagnosis Knowledge Base

## How to use these profiles at diagnosis time
1. Scan the patient's reported symptoms and, for EACH of the 22 conditions below, decide how many of its CORE symptoms are actually present.
2. Note the key DISTINGUISHERS / "deal-breaker" words for each condition (marked in bold blocks).
3. Let the MOST SPECIFIC, RARER symptom drive the choice. If the same words could mean two conditions, the condition whose distinctive features are clearly present wins (e.g., watery eyes + sneezing → allergy, not common cold; burning when urinating → UTI, not allergy).
4. Prefer condition-specific keywords over generic ones. Generic symptom-free or generic-only clues (fatigue, headache, weakness) alone should NOT pin a rare disease heavily — they only tie-break.
5. If a symptom is USUALLY the hallmark of one condition, treat that as strong evidence (e.g., jaundice/yellow skin → jaundice; blood in urine + burning → UTI; sudden severe headache with other strokes → exclude migraine and consider stroke).
6. Always append the medical disclaimer from the footer. Do NOT invent dosages, lab cutoffs, or guarantees; state it as population trends, not a replacement for a licensed clinician.
