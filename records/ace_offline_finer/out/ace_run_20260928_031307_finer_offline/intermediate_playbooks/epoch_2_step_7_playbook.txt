Matching the superficial keyword 'interest rate' to the generic stated-rate tag is the most common error in this domain. [ctx-00004] helpful=0 harmful=0 :: When a company describes selling newly issued shares to investors in a PRIMARY OFFERING (with underwriters, overallotments, or investment banks), these ARE new shares issued by the company, not sales of existing shares. Do NOT default to 'SaleOfStockNumberOfSharesIssuedInTransaction' just because the language uses 'sold' - in a primary offering context, 'sold' means the company issued new shares to investors. The key distinction: 'SaleOfStockNumberOfSharesIssuedInTransaction' is for secondary offerings where existing shareholders sell their shares, while 'StockIssuedDuringPeriodSharesNewIssues' is for primary offerings where the company issues new shares. Rely on the standard taxonomy structure and the actual transaction type rather than over-interpreting the language and creating distinctions that don't align with the taxonomy. When in doubt, check whether the company is issuing NEW shares (primary) or selling EXISTING shares (secondary) to determine the correct tag.
[ctx-00006] helpful=0 harmful=0 :: When tagging interest rates, carefully distinguish between 'stated' rates and 'effective' rates. 'Stated' rates are the nominal rates explicitly written in the loan agreement (e.g., 'interest rates range from 5% to 6.9%'). 'Effective' rates account for compounding, fees, and other factors. When a sentence simply states an interest rate range or a specific rate without mentioning compounding, fees, or effective annual rates, it refers to the STATED rate, so use 'DebtInstrumentInterestRateStatedPercentage'. Only use 'DebtInstrumentInterestRateEffectivePercentage' when the text explicitly discusses effective rates, annual percentage yields, or rates that account for compounding and fees.
[ctx-00007] helpful=0 harmful=0 :: When tagging borrowing capacity, carefully distinguish between 'maximum borrowing capacity' and 'remaining borrowing capacity'. 'Remaining borrowing capacity' is the unused portion after accounting for outstanding loans, letters of credit, etc. The phrase 'availability under this credit facility' or 'the credit facility is available for borrowing' refers to the MAXIMUM capacity, not the remaining capacity. Only use 'LineOfCreditFacilityRemainingBorrowingCapacity' when the text explicitly asks for or calculates the unused portion after deducting outstanding obligations.

[ctx-00010] helpful=0 harmful=0 :: When extracting share-related financial values, distinguish between PAR VALUE and MARKET PRICE. It is NOT the market price or trading value. Context clues that signal par value: phrases like 'par value', 'stated value', 'nominal value', 'legal capital per share', or a very small dollar amount (cents or fractions of a dollar) associated with shares. The tag 'CommonStockParOrStatedValuePerShare' captures this specific value. Do NOT confuse par value with market price, book value, or shares outstanding - these are distinct concepts. [ctx-00011] helpful=0 harmful=0 :: When tagging share counts related to equity incentive plans, distinguish between 'shares AVAILABLE FOR GRANT' and 'shares AUTHORIZED'. This maps to tags like 'ShareBasedCompensationArrangementByShareBasedPaymentAwardNumberOfSharesAvailableForGrant'. 'Shares authorized' (or 'maximum shares that may be delivered') represents the TOTAL CAPACITY/CEILING - the maximum number of shares the plan is designed to issue over its lifetime, regardless of what has been issued. The key distinction: 'available' = remaining/reserved (a subset), 'authorized' = total maximum (the whole). When a text says 'maximum number of shares that may be delivered', this indicates the authorized ceiling. This distinction prevents conflating remaining capacity with total capacity.

This rule resolves the tension with the stated-vs-effective rate distinction: the stated-vs-effective distinction applies to fixed nominal rates, while the spread-vs-stated distinction applies to variable-rate-plus-spread expressions.
