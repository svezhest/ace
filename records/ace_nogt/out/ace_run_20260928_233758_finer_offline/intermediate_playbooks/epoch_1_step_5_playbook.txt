## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

[err-00002] helpful=0 harmful=2 :: MISTAKE: Mapping a cash component of acquisition consideration to the total consideration tag. The model incorrectly reasoned that 'cash paid is a component of total consideration transferred, therefore it maps to the total consideration tag.' This conflates a component with the aggregate. CORRECTION: A component value (e.g., cash paid of $99M) must map to its specific component tag ('PaymentsToAcquireBusinessesGross'), not the aggregate tag ('BusinessCombinationConsiderationTransferred1'). Do not let the logic 'X is part of Y, so X maps to Y's tag' drive your decision - instead, ask whether the entity represents the total or just a portion, and match accordingly.
[err-00004] helpful=1 harmful=1 :: MISTAKE: Selecting a generic tag when a more specific tag exists. The model incorrectly tagged '225.0' as 'LineOfCredit' instead of 'LineOfCreditFacilityMaximumBorrowingCapacity'. The model recognized the general concept (revolving credit facility) but failed to identify that the specific context ('aggregate principal amount of up to $225.0 million') describes the MAXIMUM BORROWING CAPACITY, not just a generic line of credit. CORRECTION: Always select the MOST SPECIFIC tag that captures the exact semantic meaning of the entity, not a generic tag that could apply. Pay close attention to qualifiers like 'up to', 'maximum', 'aggregate principal amount', etc., which indicate specific financial concepts (like maximum borrowing capacity) rather than general categories. When multiple tags seem applicable, systematically compare all available tags and choose the one that most precisely describes the entity's meaning in context. Do not let the general concept override the specific qualifier.

## CONTEXT CLUES & INDICATORS

[ctx-00001] helpful=0 harmful=0 :: CRITICAL TAG DISTINCTION - Component vs. Aggregate: 'PaymentsToAcquireBusinessesGross' = gross CASH payments made to acquire businesses (a specific component). 'BusinessCombinationConsiderationTransferred1' = TOTAL consideration transferred (aggregate of cash + stock + debt + other non-cash components). When an entity represents a specific cash amount paid in an acquisition, it maps to 'PaymentsToAcquireBusinessesGross', NOT the total consideration tag. A component value should NEVER map to an aggregate tag - always match the specificity of the entity to the specificity of the tag. If the entity is the total consideration (including non-cash components), use 'BusinessCombinationConsiderationTransferred1'; if it is only the cash portion, use 'PaymentsToAcquireBusinessesGross'.
[ctx-00003] helpful=0 harmful=0 :: CRITICAL TAG DISTINCTION - Count vs. Dollar Value: When matching an entity to a tag, FIRST determine whether the entity is a COUNT (number of shares/units) or a DOLLAR VALUE (dollar amount). Tags ending in 'NumberOfShares' or 'NumberOfUnits' capture COUNTS (e.g., 'ShareBasedCompensationArrangementByShareBasedPaymentAwardNumberOfSharesAuthorized' captures the number of shares/units granted). Tags ending in 'GrantsInPeriod', 'CompensationCost', or 'FairValue' capture DOLLAR VALUES (e.g., 'EquityInstrumentsOtherThanOptionsGrantsInPeriod' captures the dollar value of grants in the period). Do NOT let the RSU-vs-option distinction override the more fundamental count-vs-dollar distinction - the model incorrectly prioritized the RSU classification over the count-vs-dollar distinction, leading it to select dollar-value tags for count entities. Always match the specificity of the entity (count vs. dollar) to the specificity of the tag (count vs. dollar).
[ctx-00005] helpful=0 harmful=0 :: CRITICAL TAG DISTINCTION - Revolving Credit Facility Total Size vs. Maximum Borrowing Capacity: When tagging revolving credit facility amounts, distinguish between the facility's TOTAL SIZE (aggregate principal amount) and its MAXIMUM BORROWING CAPACITY. The facility's total size corresponds to 'LineOfCreditFacility', while the maximum borrowing capacity (the maximum amount that could be borrowed) corresponds to 'LineOfCreditFacilityMaximumBorrowingCapacity'. Look for qualifiers like 'up to', 'maximum', 'could borrow', or 'aggregate principal amount' to determine which tag applies. The PRESENCE of a specific dollar amount alone does NOT indicate maximum borrowing capacity - it may simply describe the facility's total size. For example, 'The Company has a $75 million revolving credit facility' describes the facility's total size (aggregate principal amount), so it maps to 'LineOfCreditFacility', NOT 'LineOfCreditFacilityMaximumBorrowingCapacity'. Conversely, 'up to $225.0 million' or 'maximum borrowing capacity of $225.0 million' indicates the maximum amount borrowable, so it maps to 'LineOfCreditFacilityMaximumBorrowingCapacity'. Do not let the general concept (revolving credit facility) override the specific qualifier - the qualifier determines whether the amount represents total size or maximum capacity. When multiple tags seem applicable, systematically compare all available tags and choose the one that most precisely describes the entity's meaning in context. The absence of qualifiers like 'up to', 'maximum', or 'could borrow' suggests the amount represents the facility's total size, not its maximum borrowing capacity.
[ctx-00006] helpful=0 harmful=0 :: CRITICAL TAG DISTINCTION - Stated vs. Effective Interest Rate: When an entity describes an interest rate, distinguish between the STATED (nominal) rate and the EFFECTIVE rate. The STATED rate is the nominal rate explicitly written in the agreement or stated in the document (e.g., '8% interest rate on Seller Notes'), and it maps to tags capturing the stated/nominal rate (e.g., 'DebtInstrumentInterestRateStatedPercentage'). The EFFECTIVE rate reflects the true economic cost after adjusting for compounding, fees, or other factors, and it maps to tags capturing the effective rate. Look for qualifiers like 'stated', 'nominal', 'coupon rate', or 'as stated in the agreement' to identify the stated rate, versus 'effective', 'yield', 'effective interest rate', or 'true cost' to identify the effective rate. The PRESENCE of the word 'stated' or 'nominal' alone indicates the stated rate, not the effective rate - do not assume a stated rate is equivalent to the effective rate. Always match the specificity of the entity (stated vs. effective) to the specificity of the tag (stated vs. effective rate). When multiple tags seem applicable, systematically compare all available tags and choose the one that most precisely describes the entity's meaning in context.
